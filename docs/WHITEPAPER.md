## Abstract

PowerChain Local Energy OS is a full-stack distributed-energy operating platform that coordinates physical electricity infrastructure, verified energy evidence, local energy markets, grid operations, renewable generation, financial settlement, blockchain execution, Energy Real-World Assets, SaaS applications, and autonomous machine services.

The platform is designed for households, prosumers, consumers, energy communities, utilities, energy companies, aggregators, grid operators, renewable-energy producers, solar farms, wind farms, power plants, batteries, EV charging operators, virtual power plants, smart-meter networks, IoT and DePIN infrastructure, enterprise clients, and autonomous software agents.

> **Physical energy remains authoritative. Blockchain provides settlement, representation, interoperability, provenance, and programmable coordination.**

PowerChain does not create electricity from blockchain state. Every digital Energy RWA must trace back to verified physical-energy evidence, and every economically active representation must remain bounded by the verified physical supply from which it originated.

The canonical operating sequence is:

```text
Measure → Verify → Locate → Prove → Position → Reserve
→ Route → Trade → Deliver → Reconcile → Settle → Retire → Reward
```

## 1. Why a Local Energy Operating System

The energy system is becoming increasingly distributed. Electricity is generated by rooftop solar, community systems, wind and solar farms, batteries, conventional plants, and other distributed-energy resources. Electric vehicles can become flexible loads or distributed storage. Grid operators increasingly depend on telemetry, forecasting, flexibility, and real-time coordination.

At the same time, the digital systems surrounding electricity remain fragmented.

Meters record physical quantities. SCADA and operational-technology systems control infrastructure. Grid operators maintain electrical constraints. Market systems coordinate commercial commitments. Financial systems record receivables and settlement. Blockchains provide programmable assets and settlement rails. IoT systems provide telemetry. AI systems provide forecasting and automation.

PowerChain Local Energy OS provides a common coordination model between these systems without replacing the systems that remain authoritative for physical operations.

## 3. Canonical Energy Accounting

PowerChain uses integer watt-hours as the authoritative internal accounting unit.

```text
1 Wh  = 1 Wh
1 kWh = 1,000 Wh
1 MWh = 1,000,000 Wh
1 GWh = 1,000,000,000 Wh
```

Canonical implementation:

```ts
export type EnergyWh = bigint;

export const WH  = 1n;
export const KWH = 1_000n;
export const MWH = 1_000_000n;
export const GWH = 1_000_000_000n;
```

kWh, MWh, and GWh are denominations of the same physical-energy accounting system. They are not independent physical supplies and they are not interchangeable with PWRC.

Integer Wh accounting provides one precision model across residential, commercial, industrial, EV, storage, and utility-scale energy.

## 4. Energy Evidence

Raw telemetry is evidence, not automatically economically authoritative state.

PowerChain therefore validates physical measurements before they become available to market, RWA, settlement, or reward systems.

```text
Measurement
     ↓
Validation
     ↓
Physical Plausibility
     ↓
Quality Assessment
     ↓
Energy Proof
```

Validation can include:

- meter identity and device authentication
- timestamp and interval validation
- duplicate detection
- continuity checks
- signature verification
- calibration status
- physical plausibility
- grid-topology context
- anomaly detection
- quality scoring
- operational policy

A signed packet can still be incorrect. Cryptographic authenticity is one input to verification rather than a replacement for physical validation.

## 5. Energy Proof

An Energy Proof binds validated physical evidence to a defined energy interval.

```ts
interface EnergyProof {
  id: string;
  siteId: string;
  meterId: string;
  source: EnergySource;

  measuredWh: bigint;
  verifiedWh: bigint;

  intervalStart: Date;
  intervalEnd: Date;

  qualityScore: number;
  evidenceRoot: string;
  verificationVersion: string;
}
```

The Energy Proof creates the canonical bridge between physical telemetry and economically usable energy state.

## 6. Energy Batch

Verified Energy Proofs can be aggregated into an Energy Batch.

```text
Energy Proofs
      ↓
Energy Batch
      ↓
Energy Positions
```

Example:

```text
ENERGY BATCH ENB-FI-20260823-0042

Source                 Wind
Facility               North Ridge
Measured               132.620 MWh
Verified               130.840 MWh
Positioned              90.000 MWh
Reserved                18.000 MWh
Retired                 22.000 MWh
Available               40.840 MWh
```

A batch preserves source, location, production interval, evidence root, verified supply, issued positions, reservations, invalidations, and retirement.

Typical lifecycle:

```text
COLLECTING
→ VALIDATING
→ VERIFIED
→ FINALIZED
→ TOKENIZABLE
→ PARTIALLY_TOKENIZED
→ FULLY_TOKENIZED
→ CLOSED
```

Exceptional states may include `DISPUTED`, `CORRECTED`, and `REVOKED`.

Finalized records should not be silently mutated. Corrections create explicit revisions.

## 10. Physical-Supply Invariant

The most important Energy RWA invariant is:

```text
Economically Active Energy
<=
Verified Physical Energy
```

A more complete formulation is:

```text
Issued Energy Positions
+ Reserved Energy
+ Active Chain Representations
<=
Verified Supply
- Invalidated Supply
- Retired Supply
```

The invariant must be enforced through API validation, database constraints, market logic, Solana programs, Sui Move modules, and cross-chain representation accounting.

Example:

```text
Verified Energy        100 MWh
Solana Position         60 MWh
Sui Position            25 MWh
Reserved                10 MWh
Total Active            95 MWh
Available                5 MWh
```

This is valid.

The following must be rejected:

```text
Verified Energy        100 MWh
Solana Position        100 MWh
Sui Position           100 MWh
```

## 22. Example Local Energy Trade

A solar household produces:

```text
Measured              14.380 kWh
Verified              14.210 kWh
Local Consumption      4.900 kWh
Battery Reservation    1.000 kWh
Available              8.310 kWh
```

The prosumer offers 8.0 kWh at €0.17/kWh. A local consumer requests 8.0 kWh with a maximum price of €0.18/kWh.

PowerChain validates price, local energy availability, grid zone, and export capacity. The position is reserved and committed.

Physical metering later reports:

```text
Committed              8.000 kWh
Delivered              7.862 kWh
Variance               0.138 kWh
```

Financial settlement uses the reconciled delivered quantity. Blockchain settlement does not override the meter result.

## 40. Degraded-Service Policies

PowerChain should preserve safe operation when optional infrastructure becomes unavailable.

```text
Solana unavailable
→ preserve pending settlement
→ prevent duplicate submission

Sui unavailable
→ pause bridge completion
→ preserve source-chain state

Oracle unavailable
→ stop price-sensitive actions

Telemetry stale
→ mark evidence stale
→ block settlement when required

PWRC rewards unavailable
→ continue physical-energy operations

Enterprise integration unavailable
→ queue synchronization
```

Optional systems must not automatically stop critical physical-energy operations.

PowerChain Local Energy OS is not simply an electricity marketplace, smart-metering product, VPP, EV charging backend, blockchain, tokenization service, or SaaS dashboard.

PowerChain provides a common operating model for physical energy, verified evidence, local markets, grid operations, Energy RWA, financial settlement, Solana/SVM, Sui, stablecoin interoperability, machine payments, supply-chain provenance, enterprise applications, and autonomous agents.

## 49. Canonical Principle

**PowerChain coordinates energy; it does not invent energy.**

Every Energy RWA originates from verified physical supply.

Every economically active Energy Position remains bounded by that supply.

Every cross-chain representation preserves the same backing.

Every settlement remains traceable to delivery and reconciliation.

Every PWRC and wPWRC balance remains economically distinct from kWh and MWh Energy RWA.

That separation is the foundation of the PowerChain Local Energy OS v1.0.0 architecture.
